# LR-0002 — Annotating is recognition; only retrieval moves anything

**Date:** 2026-09-23 · **Confidence:** high (four independent instances) · **Supersedes:** the assumption that reviewing his annotated decks is useful study

## The observation
Four times now, Jayroop has failed to retrieve a concept whose correct answer is written in his own handwriting on the very slide the question came from.

| Concept | His margin note | What he produced under retrieval |
|---|---|---|
| GINI extremes | (annotated correctly) | Placed the maximum at a pure node — inverted |
| Precision / recall | (annotated correctly) | Swapped the two, twice, from unrelated angles |
| Interval vs ratio | *"I can't say Ali is twice Ahmed cause my ref point is not 0"* | Called kg "interval (no absolute zero)" |
| Lazy learner | *"Prediction you do work → lazy learner → Don't prepare ahead of time. Other Alg, Prepare model during training"* | Described Euclidean distance, drifted to dimensionality |

## Why it happens
Annotating runs on **recognition**: the text is in front of you, and the note is a reaction to it. Retrieval runs on reconstruction from nothing. The teach skill's distinction is exactly this — high *fluency* strength (it felt obvious when he wrote it) with low *storage* strength (it will not come back unprompted). The notes are evidence he understood it **once**, and no evidence at all that he can produce it in an oral interview.

## What follows
1. **Never assign deck re-reading as study.** It will feel productive and change nothing. It is the activity that produced these notes in the first place.
2. **Treat a correct margin note as a flag candidate, not a strength.** A concept he annotated well and cannot retrieve is a concept with a false sense of security attached — which is worse than a known gap, because he will not revise it.
3. **The annotations remain useful to the tutor, not to the student:** they mark exactly which concepts he has met and believes he knows, which is the highest-yield list to attack.

## The counter-example that proves the mechanism
On the same day as the lazy-learner failure, FP-1 produced a complete mechanism on its fourth attempt — the first time it was asked as **formula-reading** rather than prose. Same student, same day, same concept family. The difference was that the question forced reconstruction from a symbol on the screen rather than recall of a phrase. That is retrieval with a scaffold, and it is the format every mechanism question should now take.
